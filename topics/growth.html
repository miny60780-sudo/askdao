Personal growth: understanding yourself in a season of change

Sometimes there is no decision to make and no one to reconcile with. The question is quieter: Who am I becoming, while everything around me changes?

First, separate a transition from a failure

Seasons of change often feel like falling behind — old interests fade before new ones form, old certainties dissolve before new ones settle. It is easy to read that in-between as a defect in yourself. But a transition is not a verdict. A field between harvests is not a failed field; it is a field in the part of the cycle where nothing shows above the surface. What you are experiencing may be exactly that: real change, happening at a depth that is not yet visible.

An older way of looking at it

The Chinese word at the heart of the I Ching — Yi (易) — simply means "change". The book's founding observation is that change is not an interruption of life; it is the substance of it. Its companion text, the Daodejing, adds the practice of wuwei — often mistranslated as "doing nothing", but closer to action without forcing: tending what is growing without tearing it out of the ground to check the roots. Applied to a person, the idea is plain: growth has a pace that effort can support but not command. Your task in a changing season is less to manufacture a new self than to notice, honestly, which parts of the old one you have already outgrown.

Three questions worth sitting with

What am I still defending that I no longer actually believe?

If a friend described my exact situation, what would I notice that I cannot see from inside it?

What small, real step belongs to this season — not the next one?
