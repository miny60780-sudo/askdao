Work: when things stall, or change is forced on you

A stalled project, a blocked promotion, a job that used to fit and no longer does — few things wear a person down like work that will not move.

First, separate the season from your worth

When work stalls, the mind reaches for a verdict about itself: I am failing. But a stall is an observation about a situation, not a judgment about a person. Teams lose funding, markets turn, managers change. Some of what is stuck is yours to move; some of it is weather. Telling the two apart is the first honest step — and it is hard to do from inside the frustration.

An older way of looking at it

The I Ching has a hexagram for exactly this weather: Pi (否, Standstill) — heaven and earth not meeting, things blocked. The old text does not pretend blockage is failure; it treats it as a phase in a cycle, the way winter is a phase and not a verdict on the year. In a standstill, the tradition's counsel is consolidation over display: keep what is essential in good order, spend little, and prepare for the turn rather than forcing a door that is not ready to open. Forcing has its own costs — and so does waiting past the moment. The question the book keeps asking is not "how do I push harder?" but "what season is this, and what does this season actually reward?"

Three questions worth sitting with

Which part of this stall is within my control, and which part is weather?

Am I trying to solve the problem — or to prove that it is not my fault?

If this is a winter phase, what should I be quietly preparing for spring?

None of this replaces practical judgment, an honest conversation, or professional advice where those are needed. It is a framework for looking. See also The Book of Changes and our Sources & Approach.
