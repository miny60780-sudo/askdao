Relationships: when understanding each other becomes difficult

Relationships can become difficult when what we experience and what we believe it means begin to blur together.

First, separate what happened from what it means

When someone you care about becomes distant, you may find yourself searching for an explanation. Perhaps they no longer care. Perhaps you did something wrong. Perhaps the relationship itself is changing. These thoughts can feel convincing, especially when emotions are strong. But what happened and what you believe happened are not always the same thing. Silence is an observation; rejection is one possible interpretation — there may be other things happening that you cannot yet see. You are facing a difficult situation. That does not define your worth, and it does not mean you must solve everything immediately.

An older way of looking at it

The I Ching often explores the relationship between opposing forces, changing conditions, and the importance of timing. Its philosophy invites us to look beyond a fixed position. In a relationship, this might mean considering not only what each person wants, but also how their actions affect one another, and whether the present moment allows for genuine understanding at all. Flexibility, however, does not mean abandoning your own boundaries. Understanding another person and protecting your own dignity can both matter — seeing the other side is not the same as tolerating harm.

Three questions worth sitting with

What do I know from experience, and what am I assuming?

If I stepped outside my own perspective for a moment, what else might I notice?

What do I need from this relationship — and what am I no longer willing to accept?
